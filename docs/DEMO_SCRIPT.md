# MEHFIL demo script

**Length:** 2:30. Record at 1080p, phone and desktop both in frame at least once. No trademarked music, no brand logos. The groom portrait is consented. Say the APIs out loud.

**On screen the whole time:** the running app, not slides, except a one-second end card with the URL.

## 0:00–0:18 — The week

**Picture:** Landing. The three live frames: mustard kurta, ivory sherwani, navy prince coat. Hold on the face so it is obvious it is one person.

**Voiceover:**
“A South Asian wedding is a week. Mehndi, Baraat, Walima. The clothes change. The makeup has to belong to each one. And if you bought the outfit online, you have never seen it on your own face. This is MEHFIL.”

## 0:18–0:40 — Open the groom week

**Picture:** Click “Open the groom week.” The stylist trace ticks through with the unit counter stuck at zero. Point at “makeup, then each outfit.”

**Voiceover:**
“This groom week is a real YouCam run, on a photo used with consent. The stylist is not guessing. Skin Analysis, standard quality, seven concerns, read an overall score of 89. Dark circles are the one score that stands out, a display 63. Facial Color Tones measured the skin at hex 805E46. We hide the hair color name. On this run it was wrong.”

## 0:40–1:05 — The three looks

**Picture:** Looks carousel. Drag the before/after on Mehndi. Swipe to Baraat. Toggle “Outfit first, then makeup.” Swipe to Walima.

**Voiceover:**
“Clothes VTO version 4 put each garment on the portrait. The category is upper body, because this photo is chest-up. Makeup VTO then, or before, depending on the person. For a groom, one grooming look is enough, so makeup runs once and each outfit follows. That is cheaper. For a bride, makeup is different every night, so it runs after the outfit. We tried both on the sherwani. Outfit first keeps more real skin. Makeup first is the shared groom chain. The card lets you see both.”

## 1:05–1:28 — Skin goal

**Picture:** Skin page. The zoomed slider on the eyes. Tap the dark-circle mask. Show the countdown and the three prep lines.

**Voiceover:**
“Skin Simulation is the event-day goal, on a face crop. The crop matters. If the face is too small in the frame, skin analysis, color tones, and simulation fail. The change is subtle, so the slider is on the eyes, not a fantasy of new skin. The countdown is cosmetic. Sleep, a cool compress, don’t start a new product this week. It is not a diagnosis.”

## 1:28–1:50 — Shades and the look book

**Picture:** Shade list. Add the veil and the under-eye softener. Open the look book. Hit print, or show the downloaded card.

**Voiceover:**
“The shade list is generic on purpose. A sheer veil in the measured skin hex. An under-eye softener a little lighter. No brand names, no logos. The look book is one page you can print or save.”

## 1:50–2:18 — Your photo, and the other roles

**Picture:** Quickly: capture screen with the quality checks and the two crops, full photo and face crop. Then the role switch, Bride, and a placeholder card with the word PLACEHOLDER readable. Back to the groom Baraat frame.

**Voiceover:**
“Use my photo runs the same chain live, after the browser checks the picture, and only twice a day per person, under a unit ceiling. The demo you just watched spent zero. Bride and guest are in the product, and until we have consent they are drawings, marked placeholder, not fake people. The groom path is the one that had to be real, because the face is ours.”

## 2:18–2:30 — Close

**Picture:** The three frames again. End card: MEHFIL, and the five API names in small type: Skin Analysis, Facial Color Tones, Clothes VTO v4, Makeup VTO, Skin Simulation.

**Voiceover:**
“MEHFIL. The wedding week, on one face. Skin Analysis, Facial Color Tones, Clothes VTO, Makeup VTO, and Skin Simulation. A preview. Not a promise.”
